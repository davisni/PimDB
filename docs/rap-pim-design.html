The big picture

NetSuite keeps the physical part, the stock and the commercial decision to sell it somewhere at a price. The PIM keeps everything a listing says and the evidence behind it. The transport only carries finished messages between them and the channels.

Because the transport holds no rules, Versori can later be replaced by our own scripts without touching the PIM. Orders and stock never pass through the PIM, so if the PIM is down, selling continues.

Five words to agree on

Item NetSuite
The physical part on the shelf. One per part, whatever name it is sold under.
Branded product PIM
The item as sold under one of our brands for one listing angle, such as an equipment make or a reference number. It has its own part number.
Listing PIM
One page on one channel, such as an ASIN. Either owned (we wrote it) or joined (someone else's page).
Offer NetSuite
Our sale on one listing, on one channel, from one seller account: seller SKU, price, shipping template. Built beside today's Ecom Record.
Fact PIM
One piece of information about an item, stored with its source, confidence and approval status.

Brand, seller and shipper are three different things

An Amazon product page shows three names. The offer record carries all three as separate fields.

Seller account and fulfilment are choices we make. Brand on listing is filled in for us: from the seller account's brand name on an owned listing, and from the page itself on a joined one.

Left off on purpose

  • Titles, bullets, descriptions, images and fitment. They live in the PIM.
  • Sales figures. They belong in reporting.
  • Price floor and ceiling, and stock quantity rules. Parked for a later discussion.
  • Pack quantity. A set of three is sold as its own item with its own SKU.

The seller SKU is the branded product's part number. When an order arrives with RAP-MB2054-KUB on it, the offer record says that means one MB-2054 from the shelf.

Two things someone must decide

Each of the four branded products needs its own part number and UPC, or an exemption, before a marketplace will accept it as a separate product.

RAP/Kubota and RAP/New Holland are physically the same blade under the same brand. Marketplaces have rules against duplicate listings of one product. Whoever owns account health should approve the split-by-make approach before it is used widely.

Item kits as aliases

Today a NetSuite kit containing one of an item is sometimes used as an alias. In this design the offer record does that job, because it already says which item a seller SKU means. Kits stay useful for what they were made for: a real bundle, such as a set of three blades sold as one unit. This is a recommendation and still an open question.

The match check

Before the offer goes live, the PIM compares what the page claims with what we know about our item. If the page promises something our part does not deliver, the return comes to us.

Page claimsOur approved factsResult
Length 20-1/2 in20.5 inMatch
Centre hole 5/8 in5/8 inMatch
Fits Kubota 60 in deckFits 3 Kubota deck models (approved)Match
Fits John DeereNo John Deere fitment on fileNeeds review
Set of 3Our item is 1 bladeBlocked

Here the check stops the offer. To join this page we would have to sell a kit of three blades, and someone would have to confirm or reject the John Deere claim.

Risks specific to joined listings

Adding an offer says we are selling exactly the product on the page, including its brand. Joining a generic page is routine. Joining a page under another company's registered brand with a different-branded part invites counterfeit complaints, so those need an approval step.

Two of our own seller accounts offering on the same page should be checked against Amazon's multiple-account rules before we rely on it.

How a fact is stored, reused and changed

A listing does not hold its own copy of the information. It is assembled from the item's approved facts each time it is built. That is why one correction reaches every listing that depends on it.

What happens when version 2 is approved

  1. The PIM finds every listing that uses this fact: the 4 Kubota listings.
  2. It rebuilds those 4 and checks them against each channel's rules.
  3. The transport sends the 4 updates. The 4 New Holland listings are untouched.
  4. The PIM saves a snapshot of exactly what was sent and which fact versions it was built from.

Those snapshots answer two questions later: what did this listing say on the day of that order, and which listings ever used a fact we now know was wrong.

The tables behind this

Fact One row per version. Item, what it describes, make tag, value, source, confidence, who approved it, current or replaced.
Source The evidence for a fact: a vendor file and page, a web address with the quoted passage, or a person's note.
Listing Channel, account, branded product, owned or joined, publish status, channel ID.
Listing snapshot What was sent, when, and the fact versions used to build it.

This is the shape, not the final database design. That comes next.

Images

Today pictures go into a OneDrive folder named with the item's NetSuite internal ID. A pipeline converts and resizes them, hosts them on Cloudflare and writes the web addresses back to NetSuite. Most of that can stay. What changes is where the addresses are recorded and how much is known about each picture.

flowchart LR
  F[OneDrive folder
named by item ID] --> P[Image pipeline
convert and resize]
  P --> C[Cloudflare
hosts the files]
  C -->|web address| R[PIM image record
role, order, source, approval]
  R --> L[Listing picks images
to suit the channel]
  L --> T[Transport] --> CH[Channel]
  • The files stay on Cloudflare. The PIM stores the web address and information about the picture, not the picture itself.
  • An image is treated like a fact. Each has a record: which item, its role (main photo, angle, dimension drawing, in use), its order, where it came from, and whether it is approved.
  • Images belong to the item by default. A picture can instead be attached to a branded product when it shows brand packaging or a brand label.
  • Each listing chooses from the approved set. Channel rules decide how many, in what order and what the main image must look like. These are checks in the PIM, the same as text rules.
  • Joined listings use the page's own images. We supply none.
Why the source of a picture matters

A picture copied from another seller's or a manufacturer's website may belong to someone else, and may show a manufacturer's logo. Recording where each picture came from lets a reviewer catch those before they are published.

The smallest change to today's process is to have the pipeline's last step write to the PIM as well as NetSuite. Uploading through a PIM screen can replace the OneDrive folder later.

The life of a new item

  1. NetSuiteA bare item is created. It syncs to the PIM straight away so research can start.
  2. NetSuiteOffers are created in bulk by rule or spreadsheet: which brands, channels and makes. Pricing is queued for the pricing team and runs in parallel.
  3. PIMVendor data is loaded exactly as received and turned into facts labelled with the vendor and date. The item is scored for how researchable it is.
  4. PIM · agentsAgents first confirm the part's identity, then propose missing facts with sources. Agents never write live data.
  5. PIM · peopleLow-risk facts are approved by rule. Fitment and specs go to a reviewer.
  6. PIMEach listing is built from approved facts and checked against that channel's rules. Gaps go back to the work queue.
  7. TransportWhen content is approved, the price is set and the offer is active, the finished listing is delivered to the channel.
  8. ChannelThe channel accepts or rejects it. Its ID or its error message is written back to the PIM, and the status shows on the offer in NetSuite with a link to the PIM page.
  9. NetSuiteIf a customer later reports a problem, customer service adds an issue record to the offer. It lands in the PIM review queue next to the facts that were live at the time.

Shipping templates

A shipping template is a named set of delivery rules that lives inside a seller account: which regions we ship to, how fast, and what the customer pays. Each offer is assigned one template. Amazon and Walmart both call them shipping templates. eBay has shipping policies and Shopify has shipping profiles, which do the same job.

This is how "RAP ships faster than Stevens Lake" becomes real. The RAP account has a 2-day template, the SLP account has a slower one, and each offer points to the right one. The delivery promise shown to the customer comes from the template, and so do late-shipment penalties if we miss it.

  • The template name belongs on the offer record, next to price.
  • The templates themselves are set up once per seller account, by hand, in each seller portal.
  • An offer naming a template that does not exist in that account will be rejected, so the PIM should keep a list of valid template names per account and check against it.

The servers, in plain terms

A VPS is a rented slice of a computer in a data centre. Everything is reached through one front door. Nothing behind the door can be reached from the internet directly.

21 December and after

Rithum ends on 21 December 2026 with no overlap. Moving orders, stock and price to Versori is a separate project. The PIM is built in parallel and does not block it. Four PIM tasks still share that deadline.

Before 21 DecemberWhy it cannot wait
Export everything from Rithum: templates, defaults, mappings, lookup lists, listing IDs, SKU mappingsAccess is assumed to end with the contract. This is the raw material for the PIM's rules and for loading today's listings.
Build one complete map of every live channel SKU to its NetSuite itemThe Versori cutover needs it to route orders. The PIM is built on the same map.
Leave the current eCom record alone and build the new offer record beside itThe cutover depends on the current record.
Decide how new listings and content edits happen until the PIM is readyAfter Rithum there is no content pipeline. Options: freeze, edit by hand in seller portals, or a basic Versori flow.

Bringing today's listings into the PIM

  1. Pull every live listing from each channel, plus the Rithum export and the NetSuite eCom records. Load each as facts labelled with where they came from.
  2. Register each existing listing in the PIM as legacy. The PIM sends nothing to a legacy listing, so nothing changes on the channels.
  3. Work through listings by revenue and return rate. When a listing's content is verified and approved it becomes managed, and the PIM starts sending its content.
